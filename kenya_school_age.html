1.Forecasting Kenya’s School-Age Population (2020-2040) to Inform CBC Infrastructure Planning

Title: Kenya School-Age Population Forecasting for CBC Infrastructure Planning (2020-2035): Implications for Primary, JSS and SSS Classroom Demand

Kenya is implementing the Competency-Based Curriculum (CBC), which restructured the education system into Pre-primary (3-5), Primary (6-11), Junior Secondary (12-14) and Senior Secondary (15-17).

This study uses KNBS population projections from 2020-2035 to forecast how many children will be in each of these school-age groups in the future.

Why it matters:

Population growth directly determines how many classrooms, teachers, desks and labs the government must build. If we know today that 6-13 year-olds will cross 10 million by 2032, or that Senior Secondary will need 11,856 extra classrooms by 2035, planning can start early instead of reacting to overcrowding.

The report therefore links demography to education planning showing which age group grows fastest, when the pressure is highest, whether growth is slowing, and how gender parity will change.

We are forecasting future learners to advise where and when to build schools for CBC.

3. METHODS USED IN DATA CLEANING

Data was obtained from the Kenya Data Portal hosted on kenya.opendataforafrica.org.

3.1 Data Extraction

The portal provides the dataset Kenya School-age Population Projection, 2020-2035 with 21 time series. On the portal interface we selected:

  • Sex: 3 categories (Female, Male, Total) – checked as 1,2,3 in the R snippet
  • Age: 7 categories (3-5, 6-11, 12-14, 15-17, 14-17, 6-13, etc.) – checked as 1,2,3,4,5,6,7
  • Time: Annual frequency (A), mode = All data (2020-2035)

Two extraction methods were available:

1. Direct Download: Export → Excel/CSV

2. API via R: Provided code Knoema('ifsmqkf', list('Age'='1;2;3;4;5;6;7','Sex'='1;3;M'), host='kenya.opendataforafrica.org') which requires Knoema R Package.

We used the Excel download for this analysis.

3.2 Initial Data Structure

Original file was in wide format: - Rows: Combination of Sex x AgeGroup - Columns: Years 2020, 2021, 2022…2035 as separate columns - Values: Population counts

This format is not suitable for analysis in R (tidyverse requires long format).

3.3 Cleaning Steps in R

library(readxl) library(tidyverse)

Step 1: Load raw file raw <- read_excel(“kenya-school-age-population.xlsx”)

Step 2: Check structure and missing values glimpse(raw) colSums(is.na(raw))

Step 3: Rename columns for consistency raw <- raw %>% rename(AgeGroup = Age, Sex = Sex)

Step 4: Transform from wide to long (TIDY data principle) This is the most important cleaning step

tidy <- raw %>% pivot_longer( cols = 2020:2035, names_to = “Year”, values_to = “Population” )

%>% mutate( Year = as.numeric(Year), Population = as.numeric(Population), AgeGroup = as.factor(AgeGroup), Sex = as.factor(Sex) )

Step 5: Create separate dataset for Total only analysis Many questions (Q2-Q6, Q12-Q15) require Total, not disaggregated by sex tidy_total <- tidy %>% filter(Sex == “Total”)

Step 6: Keep gender dataset for Q8 and Q9 (GPI) tidy_gender <- tidy %>% filter(Sex %in% c(“Male”,“Female”))

Step 7: Validation checks

  1. Check for duplicates tidy %>% get_dupes(AgeGroup, Sex, Year)

  2. Check for negative or zero populations (should be none) filter(tidy, Population <= 0)

  3. Check unique values unique(tidy\(AgeGroup) unique(tidy\)Sex) range(tidy$Year) # Should be 2020-2035

Step 8: Remove duplicates if any tidy <- tidy %>% distinct()

Packages: tidyverse (dplyr, tidyr, ggplot2), readxl, Knoema, broom, scales

Principles Applied: Tidy Data principles (each variable a column, each observation a row)

5: SUMMARY OF FINDINGS, CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

5.1 Summary of Findings per Objective

Q1-Q2: Growth Trends Kenya’s school-age population continues to grow but at rapidly decelerating rates. Total school-age population (3-17) grew by 6.4% in 2020-2025 but only 1.8% projected in 2030-2035. Growth is front-loaded, with 55-63% of all growth to 2035 occurring in the first five-year period. This confirms Kenya is in late demographic transition.

Q3-Q4: Primary (6-11) Demand Primary population increased from 7.17M in 2020 to 7.56M in 2035, an additional 387,000 learners (+5.4%). This requires 9,675 extra classrooms at 40:1 by 2035, of which 8,575 (89%) are needed by 2030. Growth after 2030 is negligible (+44k), meaning primary infrastructure built by 2030 will be sufficient until 2040.

Q5: Demand Shift (3-5 to 12-14 to 15-17) A structural shift from lower to upper levels is occurring. Pre-primary share declined from 33.8% to 32.5%, while senior secondary share increased from 31.0% to 32.2%, converging to parity by 2035. The education pyramid is becoming rectangular. Pre-primary will experience absolute decline after 2030 (-20k), creating ECD teacher surplus.

Q6-Q7: Gender Difference & GPI Male population slightly exceeds female across all age groups and years (Sex Ratio 100.7-102.4, GPI 0.976-0.993). Female is never higher than male in school-age population. All GPI values lie within UNESCO parity range (0.97-1.03) and remain stable 2020-2035, with marginal decline in SSS (0.993→0.982). Population parity is achieved; enrollment parity remains the challenge (enrollment GPI 0.89 in SSS vs population GPI 0.99).

Q8: Peak Period for SSS (14-17) Period 2020-2025 recorded highest increase in SSS: +260,000 (+6.4%) vs +130,000 (+3.0%) in 2025-30 and +80,000 (+1.8%) in 2030-35. Over 55% of total SSS growth to 2035 happened by 2025. The SSS crisis coincides with first CBC senior intake in 2026, creating double pressure.

Q9: 100% Transition to SSS Achieving 100% transition requires capacity for 4.52M (14-17) by 2035, +470,000 vs 2020 baseline (11,750 classrooms). Accounting for current 78% transition rate, cumulative need is 1.36M places (470k growth + 891k backlog), equivalent to 34,000 classrooms. However, 891k backlog can be absorbed through reuse of primary classrooms, double-shift, and TVET integration, reducing new build need to ∼26,750 classrooms (KES 40.1B).

Q10: 2040 Forecast Linear extrapolation to 2040 overestimates population by 70,000-130,000 per cohort (e.g., 3-5: 3.45M linear vs 3.38M realistic) because it violates declining growth assumption. Linear model projects +365,000 learners 2035-2040 requiring 9,125 classrooms that would remain underutilized. Logistic/quadratic or KNBS cohort-component models are recommended.

Q11: Cohort Dependency (6-11 → 12-14) Cohort elasticity is 0.98 (R²=0.996): 1% growth in primary (6-11) yields 0.98% growth in JSS (12-14) six years later, with survival ratio 0.51. JSS demand is predictable six years in advance. Current JSS congestion is lagged effect of 2020-2025 primary surge (+3.19%); future JSS growth 2031-2036 will be only +1.51% as primary growth slows to +1.54%, indicating temporary shortage.

5.2 Overall Conclusion

  1. Demographic pressure is peaking NOW, not in 2035. For all levels, 2020-2025 was the period of maximum absolute growth. Post-2030, school-age population stabilizes, with ECD declining. Kenya has a 10-year window (2025-2035) to build, then shift to quality.

  2. Primary is solved by 2030, secondary is the new frontier. Primary needs 8,575 classrooms by 2030 and only 1,100 more to 2035. Senior secondary needs 11,750 classrooms for growth but 26,750 for true 100% transition. Investment must pivot from primary to JSS/SSS after 2027.

  3. Gender parity in numbers is achieved, but not in completion. Population GPI 0.97-0.99 means infrastructure can be gender-neutral, but retention interventions for girls in JSS/SSS (pregnancy, STEM role models) remain critical as enrollment GPI is only 0.89.

  4. Linear planning will waste KES 13.7B. Using constant growth assumptions leads to overbuilding. Phased planning using cohort elasticity (0.98) allows TSC to predict JSS demand 6 years ahead and avoid permanent over-hiring.

5.3 Recommendations

For Ministry of Education:

1. Phase infrastructure: Build 8,600 primary classrooms 2025-2030, then stop. Build 6,500 SSS classrooms as temporary streams 2026-2029, then 3,250 permanent 2029-2032.

2. Adopt cohort-based forecasting: Use elasticity formula JSS_T+6 = Primary_T × 0.51 for annual planning, not linear trends.

3. Reuse surplus: Convert 5,000 underutilized ECD/Primary classrooms to JSS/SSS as ECD declines after 2030.

For Teachers Service Commission (TSC):

1. Cascade redeployment: ECD surplus → Primary → JSS → SSS. Freeze ECD recruitment from 2027. Retrain 5,000 ECD teachers for primary. Redeploy 15,000 qualified primary teachers to JSS (Sciences) and 8,000 JSS to SSS STEM pathway.

2. Temporary hiring: Hire 15,000 JSS teachers on 5-year contracts (2026-2031) not permanent, as JSS growth halves after 2031.

3. STEM fast-track: Train 4,000 STEM teachers/year 2026-2030 to close SSS gap for 100% transition.

For Policy:

1. Cost: Total need KES 40.1B for 26,750 SSS classrooms + KES 11.6B for primary = KES 51.7B over 10 years (KES 5.2B/year), within current budget if phased.

2. Focus shift post-2030: From building to quality – reduce pupil-teacher ratio from 40:1 to 35:1 using surplus classrooms, and invest in labs for CBC pathways.

Kenya’s school-age population is not exploding – it is plateauing. The challenge is not endless growth but managing a front-loaded surge that peaks by 2025, shifts from ECD to SSS, and becomes predictable six years in advance, offering a narrow window to achieve 100% transition through strategic redeployment rather than massive permanent construction.
